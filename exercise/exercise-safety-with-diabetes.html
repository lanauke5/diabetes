Exercise Safety With Diabetes

Category: Exercise • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

The precautions that matter most

Check glucose before activity if you use insulin or medications that cause lows, carry fast-acting carbohydrate, and wear appropriate footwear.

Those three cover the large majority of exercise-related problems.

Foot care deserves specific attention

Reduced sensation means blisters and injuries can go unnoticed. Inspect feet before and after activity.

Well-fitting shoes and clean, dry socks are not optional details for someone with neuropathy.

When to stop

Dizziness, confusion, chest pain, sudden weakness, or a glucose reading that does not respond as expected.

These warrant stopping the session and contacting your clinician.

When to get clearance first

New intense exercise, recent heart symptoms, advanced retinopathy, foot ulcers, or a very high initial reading.

A short conversation with your clinician costs far less than an avoidable injury.

"Reduced sensation means blisters and injuries can go unnoticed. Inspect feet before and after activity."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Check glucose before activity if you use insulin.
  • Carry fast-acting carbohydrate during sessions.
  • Inspect feet before and after every session.
  • Wear well-fitting shoes and clean, dry socks.
  • Stop for dizziness, chest pain, or confusion.
  • Get clearance before new intense exercise.
