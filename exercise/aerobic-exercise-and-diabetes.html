Aerobic Exercise And Diabetes

Category: Exercise • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

What aerobic exercise does to glucose

Working muscles take up glucose with less insulin, and the effect lasts for hours after the activity ends.

This is why a single walk can lower post-meal glucose, and why regular activity shifts the daily pattern.

How much is enough

Most guidance points to about 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, but any increase from a low baseline produces benefit.

The useful target is the one you can repeat next week, not the one that sounds impressive.

Intensity and duration

Moderate means you can talk but not sing. It does not require exhaustion, and exhaustion usually shortens the session.

Two shorter sessions often beat one long one, because they spread the post-activity benefit across the day.

Starting from nothing

Begin with five to ten minutes and add a little each week. The first weeks are about habit, not fitness.

If activity causes pain or dizziness, stop and discuss it with your clinician rather than pushing through.

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Start with five to ten minutes if new to activity.
  • Aim for a pace where you can talk but not sing.
  • Split sessions across the day if one long one is hard.
  • Check glucose before and after the first sessions.
  • Note the effect on your readings over two weeks.
  • Report pain, dizziness, or chest symptoms promptly.
