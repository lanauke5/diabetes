Cortisol And Blood Sugar

Category: Sleep & Stress • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

What cortisol does

It is a stress hormone that raises glucose directly and reduces insulin sensitivity, preparing the body for action.

This is a normal, useful response to acute threat. The problem is sustained elevation.

Why modern stress is different

The stress response evolved for short physical emergencies. Financial, work, and family stress lasts for months.

This is why glucose can remain shifted for weeks without any change in diet or activity.

The morning cortisol effect

Cortisol naturally peaks in the early morning, which is part of why fasting glucose is highest then.

This is a normal rhythm, not a sign something is wrong.

What lowers sustained elevation

Regular physical activity, adequate sleep, social connection, and structured relaxation each have real effects.

These work over weeks, not hours, which is why consistency is required.

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Rate stress briefly alongside your readings.
  • Look for links between hard weeks and glucose.
  • Protect sleep and activity during stress.
  • Try one relaxation practice for a month.
  • Ask your clinician if hormone testing is warranted.
  • Tell your clinician if stress is affecting your plan.
