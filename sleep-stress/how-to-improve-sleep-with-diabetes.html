How To Improve Sleep With Diabetes

Category: Sleep & Stress • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

Start with the wake time

A consistent wake time anchors the whole rhythm, and it is the one variable people can control reliably.

Fix it for two weeks before changing anything else.

Handle the nighttime glucose question

Fear of overnight lows keeps many people awake, and it is a legitimate concern for those on insulin or certain medications.

Discussing overnight targets and a pre-bed routine with your clinician addresses this directly.

Bathroom trips and sleep quality

Frequent nighttime urination fragments sleep, and it is often attributed to age rather than investigated.

If it is new or increasing, report it rather than adapting to it.

What to do if you cannot fall back asleep

Get up after about twenty minutes, do something dim and boring, and return when sleepy.

Remaining in bed awake trains the brain to associate the bed with wakefulness.

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Fix a wake time for two weeks before other changes.
  • Discuss overnight glucose targets with your clinician.
  • Report new or increasing nighttime urination.
  • Leave the bed if awake for more than twenty minutes.
  • Keep the room cool, dark, and screen-free.
  • Ask whether a sleep evaluation is warranted.
