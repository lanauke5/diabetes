Healthy Snacks For Diabetes

Category: Nutrition • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

Whether you need snacks at all

There is no rule that people with diabetes must snack. It depends on your medication, meal timing, and activity level.

If you use insulin or certain medications, snacks may be necessary to prevent lows. Ask your clinician directly.

What makes a good snack

A snack combining protein, fiber, and a little carbohydrate raises glucose slowly and holds it steady.

Plain examples include nuts with fruit, yogurt with seeds, or vegetables with hummus.

Timing matters

A snack between meals prevents a dip that leads to overeating later, and a small pre-bed snack can help those prone to overnight lows.

A snack within an hour of a meal is usually just a larger meal.

What to avoid

Refined and liquid carbohydrate alone raises glucose fast and leaves you hungry again soon after.

This is the most common snacking mistake, and the easiest to change.

"A snack combining protein, fiber, and a little carbohydrate raises glucose slowly and holds it steady."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Ask whether you need snacks given your medication.
  • Combine protein, fiber, and carbohydrate in snacks.
  • Keep two or three reliable snacks available.
  • Avoid refined carbohydrate and juice alone.
  • Note the timing that works best in your day.
  • Carry something for unexpected delays.
