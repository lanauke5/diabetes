High Fiber Foods For Diabetes

Category: Nutrition • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

Why fiber is different

Fiber is not digested into glucose, so it slows the absorption of the carbohydrate it travels with.

This is why whole fruit raises glucose far less than the same fruit as juice, despite containing similar sugar.

Soluble and insoluble

Soluble fiber, found in oats, legumes, and many fruits, forms a gel that slows glucose absorption most directly.

Insoluble fiber, found in whole grains and vegetables, supports digestion and fullness. Both are worth having.

How much to aim for

Most adults fall well short of typical recommendations. Increasing intake gradually is more comfortable than a sudden jump.

Any increase is beneficial. Going from low to moderate matters more than reaching an ideal number.

Getting more without forcing it

Legumes, whole grains, nuts, seeds, and vegetables are the highest-fiber categories. Adding one serving a day is a realistic start.

Read labels and pick the higher-fiber version within a category you already eat.

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Check the fiber content on labels you already buy.
  • Add legumes to two meals a week to start.
  • Choose whole fruit over juice whenever possible.
  • Pick whole-grain versions of the grains you already eat.
  • Increase intake gradually over weeks, not days.
  • Ask your clinician about interactions with medications.
