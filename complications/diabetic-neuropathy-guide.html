Diabetic Neuropathy Guide

Category: Complications • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 1 min read

Advertisement

What neuropathy is

Damage to nerves, usually in the feet and legs first, caused by prolonged elevated glucose and vessel changes.

It develops slowly over years, which is why it is often far advanced when first noticed.

The signs to know

Numbness, tingling, burning, or a feeling of pins and needles, most often starting in the toes.

Some people notice a loss of sensation before any pain, which is its own hazard.

Why loss of sensation is the real danger

Pain is a warning system. Without it, injuries go unnoticed and become serious.

This is why daily foot inspection is non-negotiable for anyone with neuropathy.

What can be done

Good glucose and pressure control slows progression, and treatments exist for the symptoms themselves.

Report changes early, because early attention changes the course.

"Numbness, tingling, burning, or a feeling of pins and needles, most often starting in the toes."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Report numbness, tingling, or burning promptly.
  • Inspect feet daily if sensation is reduced.
  • Never walk barefoot, even indoors.
  • Test bath water temperature before stepping in.
  • Keep glucose and blood pressure well managed.
  • Ask your clinician about symptom treatments.
