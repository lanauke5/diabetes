Circulation And Diabetes

Category: Complications • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 2 min read

Advertisement

Why circulation is affected

Elevated glucose damages small and large blood vessels over years, and this is the pathway behind most diabetes-related complications.

The process is slow, which means it is also slow to reverse or prevent.

The two kinds of vessel involvement

Small vessels are affected in the eyes, kidneys, and nerves. Large vessels affect the heart, brain, and legs.

They are monitored differently and respond to different interventions.

What actually protects circulation

Blood pressure and cholesterol control are at least as important as glucose control for large-vessel risk.

This is why your clinician focuses on all three, not glucose alone.

The signs worth reporting

Leg pain on walking that eases with rest, cold or discolored feet, sores that heal slowly, and new numbness.

These are not symptoms to wait on.

"Small vessels are affected in the eyes, kidneys, and nerves. Large vessels affect the heart, brain, and legs."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Know your blood pressure and cholesterol targets.
  • Attend every scheduled foot and eye check.
  • Report leg pain, cold feet, or slow-healing sores.
  • List all medications and take them as prescribed.
  • Ask how often lab tests should be repeated.
  • Report new numbness or skin changes promptly.
