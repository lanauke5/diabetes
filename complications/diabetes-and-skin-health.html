Diabetes And Skin Health

Category: Complications • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 1 min read

Advertisement

Why skin is affected

Elevated glucose damages small vessels and nerves, and it changes the skin environment.

This is why dryness, slow healing, and certain characteristic changes are more common.

The changes worth knowing

Slow-healing wounds, unusual darkening in fold areas, dry itchy skin, and frequent infections.

None is dangerous by itself, but each warrants a conversation.

The daily routine

Keep the skin clean and well moisturized, inspect areas you cannot easily see, and protect feet as a priority.

Moisturizing dry skin prevents the cracking that lets infection in.

What warrants a clinician

A wound that is not healing, redness spreading, warmth, drainage, or a bad odor.

These need professional attention promptly.

"Slow-healing wounds, unusual darkening in fold areas, dry itchy skin, and frequent infections."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Moisturize dry skin daily.
  • Inspect areas you cannot easily see.
  • Report wounds that are not healing.
  • Report spreading redness, warmth, or odor.
  • Prioritize foot protection and inspection.
  • Ask your clinician about any skin changes.
