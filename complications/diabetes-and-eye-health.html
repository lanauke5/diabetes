Diabetes And Eye Health

Category: Complications • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 1 min read

Advertisement

Why the eyes are affected

Small vessels at the back of the eye are among the first to show diabetes-related damage.

Early changes produce no symptoms at all, which is the central problem.

Why screening matters so much

Damage is detectable by examination before it affects vision, and early treatment preserves sight.

Waiting for symptoms means waiting too long, which is why screening intervals exist.

What a screening involves

A dilated eye examination, and sometimes retinal photography. It is quick and painless.

Ask your clinician how often you should have one.

What you can do

Attend every scheduled screening, keep glucose and blood pressure well managed, and report any vision change promptly.

Blurriness that comes on suddenly is not something to watch and wait.

"Damage is detectable by examination before it affects vision, and early treatment preserves sight."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Confirm your screening schedule with your clinician.
  • Attend every appointment, even without symptoms.
  • Report sudden vision changes the same day.
  • Know your blood pressure target.
  • List all medications and take them as prescribed.
  • Ask whether retinal photography is available.
