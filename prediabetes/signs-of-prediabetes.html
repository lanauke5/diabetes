Signs Of Prediabetes

Category: Prediabetes • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 1 min read

Advertisement

Why symptoms are the wrong test

Prediabetes typically produces no noticeable symptoms, so feeling fine means nothing about your glucose.

This is why screening, not symptom-watching, is how it is found.

The signs that do sometimes appear

Increased thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, and skin changes in fold areas are occasionally reported.

When they appear they warrant a test, but their absence is not reassuring.

Who should ask about screening

Anyone over a certain age, with a family history, higher body weight, gestational diabetes history, or inactivity.

The specific criteria vary, so ask your clinician whether screening applies to you.

The one useful action

A simple blood test answers the question definitively, and it is inexpensive and widely available.

If you have been wondering, that is reason enough to ask.

"Increased thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, and skin changes in fold areas are occasionally reported."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Ask your clinician if screening applies to you.
  • Mention any family history of diabetes.
  • Report any symptoms, even mild or vague ones.
  • Do not assume feeling well means all is fine.
  • Ask how often retesting makes sense.
  • Bring your questions in writing to the visit.
