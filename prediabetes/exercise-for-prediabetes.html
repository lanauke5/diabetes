Exercise For Prediabetes

Category: Prediabetes • By Editorial Team • Updated 2026-09-18 • 1 min read

Advertisement

Why activity works here

Working muscles take up glucose with less insulin, which directly addresses the problem behind prediabetes.

The effect lasts well beyond the activity itself.

How much is needed

About 150 minutes a week of moderate activity is the usual target, and any increase from a low baseline helps.

Consistency matters more than intensity.

Where to start

Walking after meals is the simplest and most accessible option for most people.

Start small enough that repeating it tomorrow is unremarkable.

Why this is not all-or-nothing

Some activity is far better than none, and missed weeks are not starting over.

Perfection is the enemy of a sustainable pattern.

"About 150 minutes a week of moderate activity is the usual target, and any increase from a low baseline helps."

Bring these details to your next visit

  • Start with short walks after main meals.
  • Build toward 150 minutes a week gradually.
  • Aim for a pace where you can talk but not sing.
  • Track readings if you monitor them.
  • Ask your clinician about clearance for intense exercise.
  • Resume at the last comfortable level after breaks.
